# Evidence And Red Flags

## Evidence Hierarchy

Prefer higher-quality evidence:

1. Official original documents: audit report, bank statement, tax filing, contract, invoice, acceptance document, license, court/regulatory record.
2. Third-party confirmations: customer/supplier interview, bank/lender confirmation, expert call, government/registry search, external accountant/lawyer report.
3. Internal system exports: ERP, CRM, order ledger, warehouse, payroll, project system, production records.
4. Management accounts and schedules.
5. Management interview or BP/CIM claim.
6. Media, marketing material, or hearsay.

Use management claims to form hypotheses, not conclusions.

## Cross-Checks

Revenue:

- contract/order -> delivery/acceptance -> invoice -> revenue recognition -> receivable -> collection.
- customer interview -> usage/use case -> renewal/repeat purchase -> satisfaction.
- related-party check -> former employees/relatives/shareholder-linked entities.

Growth:

- revenue growth -> order growth -> backlog -> capacity -> hiring -> capex -> working capital -> market demand.

Margin:

- gross margin -> product mix -> customer mix -> procurement cost -> labor/production yield -> pricing -> peer margin.

Cash flow:

- EBITDA -> OCF -> working capital movement -> capex -> FCF -> financing need.

Market:

- broad market report -> actual addressable segment -> customer budget -> realistic penetration -> competitor share.

Forecast:

- management plan -> historical delivery -> pipeline conversion -> customer budget -> capacity -> cash funding -> revised plan.

Operations:

- production/service claim -> capacity -> bottleneck process -> labor -> energy/logistics/packaging -> inventory -> sales.
- self-developed product claim -> R&D staff/payroll -> IP -> source/procurement records -> demo -> customer use case.

Legal/compliance:

- ownership/control claim -> business license/articles -> shareholder resolutions -> capital contribution -> equity transfer records -> pledge/freeze/dispute search.
- IP/asset claim -> certificates/registers -> renewal/annual-fee status -> license/transfer contract -> encumbrance/dispute search -> change-of-control impact.

## Red-Flag Severity

P0 deal-stopper / immediate escalation:

- Revenue authenticity cannot be verified.
- Bank, tax, contract, invoice, or acceptance evidence materially conflicts.
- Significant undisclosed debt, guarantee, pledge, litigation, regulatory, tax, environmental, or IP issue.
- Core asset/title/IP/license is invalid, disputed, or cannot transfer/use after closing.
- Founder or controller integrity concern with evidence.
- Related-party transactions or fund flows suggest tunneling, circular revenue, or off-book arrangements.
- Cash flow crisis, unpaid wages/taxes/loans, or going-concern risk not disclosed.
- Major customer/supplier dependence with imminent loss.
- Proposed valuation cannot work under any realistic revised case.

P1 price/terms/condition issue:

- Customer concentration or weak retention.
- Order growth not matched by revenue or collections.
- Margins above peers without business explanation.
- Working capital materially worse than reported earnings imply.
- Aggressive capitalization, subsidy dependence, tax irregularity, or internal-control weakness.
- Management forecast unsupported by pipeline, capacity, or customer evidence.
- Important contracts contain change-of-control, exclusivity, termination, penalty, or non-transfer clauses.
- Key-person dependency, incomplete incentives, or weak finance organization.
- Product/technology differentiation is plausible but not yet proven by customer evidence or technical benchmarks.
- Environmental, safety, labor, data, or quality issues require remediation but appear quantifiable.

P2 monitor/post-investment action:

- Reporting process immature.
- Documentation incomplete but reconcilable.
- Governance gaps common for stage.
- Finance system needs normalization.
- Non-core compliance cleanup needed.

## Report Rules

- Every P0/P1 must have a deal implication.
- Do not write "risk is controllable" unless the control mechanism is named.
- Do not write generic risks such as "market competition risk" unless tied to the target's actual exposure.
- Separate growth risk from survival/compliance risk:
  - growth risk changes valuation and return;
  - survival/compliance risk can stop or restructure the deal.

## Risk-To-Term Mapping

| Risk | Possible deal response |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue/EBITDA uncertainty | QoE adjustment, lower multiple, earnout, escrow, closing accounts, post-closing true-up |
| Working capital gap | normalized working capital peg, purchase price adjustment, cash-free/debt-free mechanism |
| Tax or compliance exposure | indemnity, escrow, specialist review, condition precedent |
| Customer concentration | valuation discount, customer confirmation CP, earnout, retention covenant |
| Founder/key-person risk | vesting, lock-up, non-compete, governance rights, key-person insurance where applicable |
| IP/title defect | closing condition, remediation before signing, legal opinion, indemnity |
| Litigation/environmental risk | specialist review, escrow, indemnity, exclusion, stop deal if unquantifiable |
| Major contract transfer/termination risk | counterparty consent, condition precedent, covenant, indemnity, carve-out |
| IP ownership or license uncertainty | legal opinion, assignment/renewal before closing, escrow, stop if core technology unusable |
| Management forecast uncertainty | revised base/downside case, lower valuation, earnout, staged investment, milestone-based funding |
