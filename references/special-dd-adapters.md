# Special DD Adapters

Use this file after classifying the transaction stage and deal type. The base checklist in `module-checklists.md` still applies, but these adapters change the emphasis, evidence threshold, and deal actions.

## 0. Adapter Selection

Choose all that apply:

| Situation | Primary DD question | Must emphasize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screening / first look | Is this worth deeper work? | thesis, market, basic financial plausibility, fatal red flags, missing materials |
| Preliminary DD | What must be true for IC? | issue tree, data-room gaps, management claims, priority interviews |
| Full DD | Can the fund invest and at what terms? | evidence matrix, QoE, legal/tax/compliance, valuation, exit, term protections |
| Term-sheet negotiation | What risk should price/terms absorb? | valuation bridge, CPs, reps/warranties, covenants, redemption, governance |
| Post-DD remediation | What must be fixed before/after closing? | remediation owner, deadline, verification evidence, post-investment plan |

## 1. VC / Early Growth Adapter

Use when the company is early, financial history is thin, and enterprise value depends on product-market fit, technology, user/customer growth, or future financing.

Minimum checks:

- Founder-market fit, founder integrity, key-person dependency, and team completeness.
- Product problem, target customer, use case frequency, willingness to pay, switching cost, and customer love.
- Technology or product edge: patents, know-how, data, workflow integration, regulatory approval, or network effect.
- Growth quality: cohort retention, churn, repeat purchase, expansion revenue, active usage, paid conversion, channel ROI.
- Burn, runway, financing need, use of proceeds, and path to next milestone.
- Customer and revenue evidence, even if small: signed contracts, pipeline, pilots, renewal, collection, references.
- Cap table, founder vesting, ESOP, historical financing terms, information rights, and liquidation preferences.
- Forecast uncertainty: show base/upside/downside milestones rather than one precise forecast.

Output emphasis:

- Investment thesis should focus on "why now", "why this team", "why this product can become large", and "what milestone unlocks the next round or exit".
- Report may simplify mature-company financial ratio analysis, but must not skip cash runway, unit economics, revenue quality, and governance terms.

## 2. Growth Equity / PE Minority Adapter

Use for companies with meaningful revenue and operating history where the investor is buying growth plus improved governance.

Minimum checks:

- Revenue bridge by product, customer, region, channel, price, volume, and contract type.
- Order-to-revenue-to-collection chain; backlog, acceptance, delivery, invoicing, credit policy, and bad debts.
- Gross margin bridge by product/customer; one-off subsidies, rebates, price concessions, and cost pass-through.
- Customer concentration, retention, wallet share, renewal, and reference checks.
- Supplier concentration, production capacity, quality incidents, and inventory aging.
- Related-party sales/purchases/funding flows and founder/company boundary.
- Normalized earnings, working capital need, cash conversion, tax normalization, and internal control gaps.
- Listing, M&A, or secondary transfer feasibility; expected hold period and fund-life fit.

Output emphasis:

- Link growth thesis to measurable operating drivers and cash conversion.
- Convert governance weaknesses into board rights, reserved matters, information rights, audit rights, and remediation covenants.

## 3. Pre-IPO Adapter

Use when investment return depends on near-term listing or when the target markets itself as "IPO-ready".

Minimum checks:

- Eligibility against the target listing venue: revenue/profit/market-cap standard, sector fit, ownership clarity, independence, and compliance record.
- Historical equity changes, capital contributions, share pledges, nominee holdings, employee holdings, and founder disputes.
- Related-party transactions and independence of assets, people, finance, business, technology, and institutions.
- Revenue authenticity, major customer/supplier dependence, gross margin reasonableness, and audit-trail completeness.
- Tax, social insurance/housing fund, environmental, safety, data, labor, and industry license compliance.
- Internal control maturity: approval flows, seals, bank accounts, contract custody, accounting policies, ERP consistency.
- Intermediary status: auditor/lawyer/sponsor engagement, known issues, rectification timeline, and listing obstacles.
- Lock-up, exit timetable, valuation downside, and what happens if listing is delayed.

Output emphasis:

- Treat "listing obstacle" as a deal gate. Separate fixable standardization issues from structural defects.
- Do not accept buyback as the main return plan. Analyze counterparty capacity and enforceability.

## 4. Buyout / M&A Adapter

Use when control, integration, purchase price adjustment, or post-closing liability allocation matters.

Minimum checks:

- Scope of acquisition: equity vs asset deal, perimeter, subsidiaries, carve-outs, minority interests, and consolidation.
- Debt-like items, cash-like items, off-balance-sheet liabilities, guarantees, commitments, and contingent liabilities.
- Normalized working capital, seasonality, closing accounts, leakage, locked-box leakage, and purchase price adjustment.
- Change-of-control clauses, customer/supplier consent, license transferability, employee retention, and key contracts.
- Standalone cost, owner compensation, related-party cost, one-off income/expense, and normalized EBITDA.
- Synergy assumptions: revenue synergy, cost synergy, timeline, implementation owner, execution risk.
- Integration: systems, people, brand, procurement, channel conflict, culture, compliance, and reporting cadence.
- Transaction protections: reps/warranties, indemnity, escrow/holdback, special indemnity, MAC, CPs, covenants.

Output emphasis:

- The report should separate "value of business" from "transaction leakage and liability allocation".
- Red flags should map directly to price adjustment, indemnity, closing condition, or deal stop.

## 5. Strategic Investment / Industrial M&A Adapter

Use when the investor seeks strategic synergy beyond financial return.

Minimum checks:

- Strategic fit: product, customer, channel, region, supply chain, technology, data, brand, or capability gap.
- Synergy realism: who captures it, what must be integrated, what contracts/people/permissions are needed.
- Governance conflict: minority protections, board seat, reserved matters, information wall, related-party pricing.
- Commercial conflict: customer overlap, channel conflict, exclusivity, non-compete, IP ownership, and data use.
- Antitrust, foreign investment, export control, sanctions, data security, and industry approval where relevant.
- Exit path if strategic synergy does not materialize.

Output emphasis:

- Separate standalone investment return from strategic option value.
- Do not double-count synergy in both valuation and post-investment upside unless the mechanism is explicit.

## 6. Debt-Like / Mezzanine / Buyback-Heavy Adapter

Use when the legal form is equity but the economic return depends heavily on redemption, repurchase, guarantee, fixed return, or VAM.

Minimum checks:

- Repurchase obligor, guarantee provider, asset coverage, cash-flow capacity, enforceability, and timing.
- Whether the arrangement creates hidden debt, regulatory, accounting, tax, or listing risk.
- Downside scenario: trigger event, dispute path, liquidity source, collateral, and expected recovery.
- Alignment risk: does the founder pursue growth or only avoid repurchase trigger?
- Whether redemption or buyback is consistent with fund mandate and target-company law/regulation.

Output emphasis:

- Label buyback/redemption as risk control, not as the base investment return path.
- If exit depends mainly on repurchase from a weak obligor, state that the investment resembles credit risk.

## 7. Commercial Factoring / Supply-Chain Finance Adapter

Use only for receivables, factoring, supply-chain finance, or asset-based financing diligence.

Minimum checks:

- Underlying transaction authenticity: contract, order, delivery, acceptance, invoice, logistics, payment trail.
- Debtor credit, concentration, dispute history, set-off rights, and payment behavior.
- Receivable ownership, pledge/transfer registration, duplicate financing, prior assignment, and legal notice.
- Related-party transactions, circular trading, shell entities, fake contracts, fake invoices, and abnormal gross margins.
- Dilution risk: returns, rebates, quality disputes, warranty claims, price adjustments.

Output emphasis:

- The core DD conclusion should be "is the receivable real, collectible, and transferable?"

## 8. Real Estate / Infrastructure / PPP Adapter

Use for land, development, infrastructure concessions, PPP, or project-company investments.

Minimum checks:

- Land title, planning, construction permits, completion acceptance, ownership certificates, and mortgage/pledge.
- Project budget, capex, contractor claims, schedule delay, funding source, and cost overrun.
- Sales/lease assumptions, pre-sale compliance, occupancy, tenant quality, and receivable collection.
- PPP/concession contract, government payment obligation, fiscal affordability, performance acceptance, and termination compensation.
- Environmental, safety, construction quality, and regulatory approvals.

Output emphasis:

- Separate asset value, project execution risk, government/tenant credit risk, and cash-flow timing risk.

## 9. Mining / Resource Rights Adapter

Use for mining rights, resource assets, and reserve-backed projects.

Minimum checks:

- License validity, reserve report, exploration/mining boundary, production permit, safety permit, and environmental approval.
- Resource grade, recovery rate, mining cost, capex, commodity price sensitivity, and mine life.
- Historical ownership transfer, pledge, litigation, community issues, land use, and restoration obligation.
- Specialist technical report and legal title review.

Output emphasis:

- State clearly which conclusions require qualified reserve, safety, environmental, or legal expert confirmation.

## 10. Overseas M&A Adapter

Use for cross-border acquisitions or targets with material overseas assets/revenue.

Minimum checks:

- Jurisdiction, local corporate law, foreign investment approval, antitrust, sanctions/export control, and FX controls.
- Local tax, transfer pricing, labor, pension, environmental, data, privacy, and anti-bribery compliance.
- Local title, contracts, litigation, government permits, and enforceability of transaction documents.
- Integration risk across language, accounting standards, systems, governance, and culture.
- Use local counsel/accountants; mark unverified areas as specialist-dependent.

Output emphasis:

- Do not overstate certainty from translated documents. Record source language, translation status, and local-advisor dependency.
