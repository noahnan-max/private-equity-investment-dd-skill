# Report Writing Patterns

Use this file when drafting the final DD report or IC memo. It gives reusable structures and phrasing patterns; adapt to the specific project and evidence.

## 1. Conclusion-First Executive Judgment

Template:

```text
Recommendation: [Proceed / Conditional proceed / Pause / Reject].

The deal is attractive because [1-3 evidence-backed reasons]. The investment case depends on [critical assumptions]. Current diligence supports [supported assumptions] but leaves [open gaps]. The largest deal risks are [P0/P1 issues], which should be handled through [evidence request / valuation adjustment / condition precedent / indemnity / covenant / post-investment action].
```

Rules:

- Do not open with company history unless the user asks for a descriptive report.
- State the recommendation, valuation/terms implication, and next action in the first page.
- Use "conditional proceed" only when the remaining work has a clear evidence path and term response.

## 2. Investment Thesis Table

| Thesis | Evidence supporting it | Evidence against it | Verification status | Deal implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Market has room for growth | market data, customer demand, policy, peer growth | market saturation, price decline, regulation | confirmed / partial / claim | affects growth forecast |
| Company has defensible edge | product, tech, customer, cost, channel | weak IP, substitutable product |  | affects multiple |
| Growth converts into cash | orders, revenue, collection, margin | receivables, inventory, churn |  | affects valuation and terms |
| Exit is feasible | IPO/M&A/secondary evidence | timing, lock-up, buyer scarcity |  | affects hold period |

## 3. Section Writing Standard

For each major section, write in this order:

1. Conclusion: what we believe.
2. Evidence: what supports the conclusion.
3. Counter-evidence or uncertainty: what could weaken it.
4. Analysis: what it means for valuation, risk, and terms.
5. Follow-up: what still needs checking.

Avoid a pure checklist dump. A checklist helps execute diligence; the report must explain judgment.

## 4. Risk Language

Weak:

```text
The company faces market competition risk.
```

Better:

```text
The company's top three customers account for [x]% of revenue, and two contracts expire within [period]. If renewal pricing falls by [x]%, FY[year] gross profit would decline by approximately [amount]. Before IC, we should complete customer calls and obtain renewal/price evidence; if unresolved, use a lower exit multiple and add customer-retention covenants.
```

Rules:

- Replace abstract nouns with the mechanism of harm.
- Link risk to evidence, financial impact, valuation, terms, or post-investment action.
- If the issue is already present, say "issue" or "impact"; reserve "risk" for uncertainty.

## 5. Scope, Assumptions, And Limitations

Use when issuing a formal report:

```text
This report is based on materials provided by the company and investor, management interviews, selected external checks, and analysis performed as of [date]. Unless otherwise stated, documents are assumed to be authentic, complete, legally executed, and consistent with the latest version provided. Areas involving legal, tax, environmental, safety, labor, data, or industry-specific regulatory conclusions require confirmation by qualified specialists. This report is prepared for internal investment decision support and should not be treated as a public investment recommendation.
```

Adjust assumptions if the materials are incomplete or if authenticity has not been verified.

## 6. IC Memo Compression

If Noah asks for an IC memo instead of a full report, compress to:

1. Recommendation and decision request.
2. Deal snapshot: company, round, amount, valuation, ownership, use of proceeds, stage.
3. Investment thesis and key evidence.
4. Core diligence findings by business, industry, financial, legal, team, valuation, exit.
5. Red flags and mitigants.
6. Return case and sensitivity.
7. Proposed terms and conditions.
8. Post-investment plan.
9. Open questions for IC.

Keep appendix-level details in evidence tables, not the main memo.

## 7. Full Report Structure Discipline

Recommended relative emphasis for a mature investment DD report:

- Executive summary and recommendation: short, decision-grade.
- Industry and competition: enough to validate market attractiveness and benchmark assumptions.
- Business/product/technology: detailed enough to show how the company creates value.
- Company/team/governance: enough to assess execution and integrity.
- Financial/QoE: detailed enough to support valuation and cash-flow judgment.
- Valuation/return/terms/risk: explicit and tightly connected to conclusion.

Do not let company background, copied BP content, or generic industry description crowd out analysis.

## 8. Bottom-Paper Discipline

For every major conclusion, preserve:

- Source document or interview.
- Date and version.
- Whether it is management claim, third-party evidence, calculated fact, or professional interpretation.
- Calculation table if numerical.
- Contradictory evidence and how it was resolved.

If the evidence cannot be preserved or described, downgrade confidence.

## 9. Final QA Checklist

Before delivering:

- The recommendation can be understood without reading the appendix.
- Every investment highlight has evidence and at least one counter-check.
- Every P0/P1 red flag has a deal action.
- Valuation uses adjusted, not merely reported, financials where diligence supports adjustment.
- Exit path is specific and compatible with fund life.
- Specialist areas are clearly marked and not overstated.
- Open questions are prioritized by impact, not listed randomly.
