# Red-Flag Playbook

Use this file to turn diligence findings into decision-grade warnings. A red flag is not just a bad fact; it is a fact or uncertainty that changes price, terms, timing, or whether the deal should proceed.

## 1. Severity

| Level | Meaning | Typical deal action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0 Deal stopper | The thesis is broken or legal/compliance defect may make the deal impossible | reject, pause, or require full remediation before IC |
| P1 Major condition | The deal may work only with evidence, price, terms, or remediation | reprice, CP, indemnity, escrow, covenant, specialist review |
| P2 Monitor | Manageable issue that should be tracked post-investment | action plan, owner, deadline, reporting |

## 2. Risk Writing Rules

- Do not write generic risks that apply to every company. Explain the company-specific mechanism.
- If the negative event is already certain, call it an issue or impact, not a risk.
- Quantify where possible: revenue affected, margin impact, cash-flow impact, valuation sensitivity, delay length.
- Every P0/P1 item must have a requested evidence item and a deal action.
- Separate management claim, confirmed fact, inference, and open question.

## 3. Red-Flag Cards

### Revenue Authenticity

Trigger:

- Revenue growth is not supported by orders, delivery, acceptance, invoices, logistics, tax invoices, collection, or customer confirmations.

Checks:

- Trace sample transactions from contract/order to delivery, acceptance, invoice, bank collection, and accounting entry.
- Compare revenue by customer with receivables, subsequent collection, credit terms, and customer interview results.
- Test period-end transactions, round-number contracts, same-address counterparties, and unusual related parties.

Deal action:

- Pause positive recommendation until sample testing and customer confirmations are complete.
- If unresolved, reprice, require special indemnity/escrow, or reject.

### Inflated Profit / Margin

Trigger:

- Gross margin or EBITDA margin materially exceeds peers or history without a documented reason.

Checks:

- Rebuild margin by product/customer/channel.
- Test capitalization of R&D, labor, channel rebates, returns, bad debt, inventory write-down, subsidies, and one-off gains.
- Compare production/logistics/headcount/raw material consumption with reported sales.

Deal action:

- Use adjusted earnings for valuation. Require price adjustment or earnout if margin sustainability is uncertain.

### Business Packaging

Trigger:

- A low-barrier or trading business is presented as high-tech, platform, SaaS, or deep manufacturing without evidence.

Checks:

- Identify actual value-added activity: design, manufacturing, IP, data, workflow, customer ownership, or only resale/intermediation.
- Review staff roles, R&D outputs, product specs, patents, customer contracts, and supplier dependency.

Deal action:

- Reclassify valuation method and peer group. If valuation depends on the unsupported label, reprice or reject.

### Related-Party Dependence

Trigger:

- Material sales, purchases, financing, assets, leases, or personnel overlap with founder, group companies, family, or undisclosed affiliates.

Checks:

- Map counterparties by ownership, address, phone, management, bank flow, transaction pattern, and disclosure.
- Assess necessity, pricing fairness, substitutability, and independence after investment/listing.

Deal action:

- Require disclosure, cleanup, pricing policy, termination/novation, indemnity, and governance reserved matters.

### Cash Conversion / Working Capital Stress

Trigger:

- Revenue/profit grows while receivables, inventory, contract assets, or prepayments grow faster than cash collection.

Checks:

- Analyze DSO, inventory days, contract asset aging, subsequent collection, bad debt, and credit policy.
- Test whether growth requires hidden financing from suppliers, banks, or shareholder loans.

Deal action:

- Adjust valuation for working capital need; add working-capital peg, covenants, or financing condition.

### Customer Or Supplier Concentration

Trigger:

- A small number of customers or suppliers drives revenue, margin, technology access, or production continuity.

Checks:

- Review contract term, renewal, pricing, exclusivity, switching cost, change-of-control consent, and customer reference.
- For suppliers, test substitutes, lead time, price pass-through, sanctions/export restrictions, and quality dependency.

Deal action:

- Require customer/supplier confirmations, retention plan, concentration discount, or CP for contract renewal.

### IP / Technology Ownership

Trigger:

- Core technology is owned by founder personally, university, hospital, former employer, related company, or license provider.

Checks:

- Review patent/software copyright/trademark/license chain, inventor employment, assignment, encumbrance, and infringement disputes.
- Confirm whether the target can use, transfer, sublicense, commercialize, and defend the IP.

Deal action:

- Require assignment/license cleanup, IP reps, specialist opinion, and CP before closing.

### Major Contract / Change Of Control

Trigger:

- Key customer, supplier, financing, license, lease, or government contract can terminate or require consent after investment.

Checks:

- Review assignment, change-of-control, exclusivity, termination, penalty, price adjustment, and audit clauses.

Deal action:

- Add consent as CP; price downside if consent timing or outcome is uncertain.

### Tax / Social Insurance / Labor

Trigger:

- Underpaid tax, informal employment, social insurance/housing fund gaps, labor dispatch, unpaid overtime, or founder-personal payments.

Checks:

- Reconcile payroll, headcount, social insurance base, personal income tax, VAT/CIT filings, invoices, and local policy.

Deal action:

- Quantify exposure; require specialist review, escrow/special indemnity, or pre-closing remediation.

### Environmental / Safety / Industry License

Trigger:

- Missing permit, prior penalties, unsafe production, pollutant discharge issue, hazardous material handling, or license boundary mismatch.

Checks:

- Review permits, inspection records, penalties, rectification, third-party testing, EIA, safety production records, and license scope.

Deal action:

- Treat as P0/P1 depending on shutdown risk. Require specialist verification and remediation CP.

### Litigation / Guarantees / Hidden Liabilities

Trigger:

- Litigation, arbitration, administrative penalty, guarantee, pledge, shareholder loan, contingent payment, or off-balance obligation.

Checks:

- Search public databases, bank letters, credit reports, contracts, board minutes, related-party confirmations, and legal counsel memo.

Deal action:

- Special indemnity, escrow, debt-like adjustment, CP for release, or reject if liability threatens solvency/control.

### Management Integrity

Trigger:

- Inconsistent statements, withheld documents, abnormal related parties, aggressive accounting, reputation issues, or previous disputes.

Checks:

- Cross-check interview statements against documents, third-party sources, customers, suppliers, employees, and historical records.

Deal action:

- Escalate severity. Integrity issues can become P0 even when financial impact is hard to quantify.

### Forecast Unsupported

Trigger:

- Forecast assumes growth, margin expansion, capex efficiency, financing, or listing without bottom-up evidence.

Checks:

- Tie forecast to customer pipeline, capacity, pricing, headcount, R&D milestone, regulatory milestone, and historical conversion.

Deal action:

- Replace with adjusted diligence forecast; use milestone tranches, earnout, or lower valuation.

### Exit Infeasible

Trigger:

- IPO, M&A, secondary sale, or buyback path is vague, delayed beyond fund life, or dependent on weak obligor.

Checks:

- Listing requirements, comparable buyers, strategic rationale, shareholder transfer restrictions, lock-up, repurchase capacity.

Deal action:

- Reconsider investment suitability; require lower entry valuation, clearer exit rights, or reject.
