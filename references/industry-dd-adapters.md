# Industry DD Adapters

Use these adapters after reading the BP/CIM and initial materials. The goal is to avoid applying the same generic diligence lens to every company.

## 0. Pick The Economic Engine

First classify what creates value:

| Economic engine | Examples | Main evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product / technology edge | semiconductor, equipment, biotech, medical device, industrial software | technical validation, customer qualification, IP, roadmap |
| Network / data / platform | marketplace, SaaS, consumer internet, data product | retention, cohorts, usage, CAC/LTV, data rights |
| Manufacturing / scale | industrial parts, consumer goods, materials | capacity, yield, cost curve, quality, supply chain |
| Project / engineering | EPC, environmental projects, systems integration | backlog, acceptance, receivables, project margin |
| Distribution / channel | consumer, medical distribution, enterprise channel | sell-through, rebates, channel inventory, repeat purchase |
| Asset / concession | real estate, infrastructure, mining, energy assets | title, permit, asset quality, cash-flow contract |

Then adapt the diligence depth. For example, an early biotech company may require far more technology/regulatory diligence than historical profit analysis, while a project engineering company may require aggressive receivable and acceptance testing.

## 1. Hard Tech / Advanced Manufacturing

Typical modules:

- Product definition, function, structure, working principle, application scenario, and key technical indicators.
- Technical moat: patents, know-how, process recipes, data, testing capability, talent, or customer certification.
- Commercial readiness: prototype, pilot, mass production, yield, reliability, delivery record, after-sales.
- Customer qualification cycle, sample testing, design-in/design-win status, and switching cost.
- Cost roadmap, BOM, yield improvement, capacity expansion, capex plan, and supply constraints.
- Localization/import substitution claim: real substitution target, customer validation, price/performance gap, policy dependence.

Verification:

- Interview technical lead, production lead, quality lead, customers, and independent technical expert.
- Request test reports, customer qualification documents, patents, product specs, failure/return records, and production records.

## 2. Semiconductor / Electronics

Focus areas:

- Business type: chip design, IDM, foundry, equipment, materials, EDA/IP, module, distributor.
- Product lifecycle: tape-out, sample, certification, mass production, design-in, design-win, customer production ramp.
- Capacity and supply: wafer/foundry allocation, packaging/testing capacity, key equipment/material availability.
- Yield, defect rate, reliability, return rate, and inventory obsolescence.
- Customer concentration, end-market cycle, substitution risk, and price erosion.
- R&D capitalization/expense policy, inventory write-down, warranty provisions, and gross margin sustainability.
- Export control, sanctions, key supplier restrictions, and localization policy.

Red flags:

- "国产替代" narrative without customer qualification or shipped revenue.
- High revenue with weak collection, channel stuffing, or distributor inventory build-up.
- Large inventory growth ahead of uncertain customer orders.

## 3. Pharma / Biotech / Medical Device

Focus areas:

- Asset type: discovery, preclinical, clinical, registration, approved product, commercial product.
- Pipeline stage, indication, mechanism, clinical endpoint, trial design, comparator, safety profile, and regulatory path.
- IP: core patents, expiry, freedom to operate, licensing, ownership with universities/hospitals/founders.
- Approval, registration, GMP/GSP, production license, MAH, adverse event, and quality system.
- Reimbursement, tendering, hospital access, distribution, rebates, channel inventory, returns, and compliance.
- R&D spending, capitalization, milestone payments, license revenue, and one-off income.
- Founder/scientist dependency and medical/commercial team completeness.

Red flags:

- Valuation based on distant pipeline milestones without probability adjustment.
- Sales growth driven by channel stock or noncompliant promotion.
- Key IP not owned by the target or subject to unresolved licensing restrictions.

## 4. SaaS / Software / Platform / Internet

Focus areas:

- Revenue type: subscription, usage, license, implementation, maintenance, transaction commission, advertising.
- ARR/MRR, NRR/GRR, churn, renewal, expansion, cohort retention, ARPU, DAU/MAU, paid conversion, CAC, LTV.
- Implementation revenue vs recurring revenue; deferred revenue and revenue recognition.
- Gross margin: cloud cost, implementation labor, customer success, support, and third-party software.
- Sales efficiency: payback period, pipeline conversion, channel commissions, enterprise sales cycle.
- Data rights, privacy, security, algorithm/IP ownership, and platform dependency.
- Product stickiness: workflow depth, integrations, switching cost, user frequency, and mission-criticality.

Red flags:

- ARR includes one-off services or unsigned pipeline.
- High growth bought by unsustainable customer acquisition spend.
- Usage metrics rise while payment, retention, or collection remains weak.

## 5. Manufacturing / Industrial Products

Focus areas:

- Product line, customer industries, pricing mechanism, BOM, raw material exposure, and cost pass-through.
- Capacity, utilization, bottleneck, yield, scrap, energy, labor, equipment maintenance, and capex need.
- Quality system, customer audit, return/claim history, warranty, and safety/environmental compliance.
- Supplier concentration, single-source risk, payment terms, inventory aging, and obsolete stock.
- Customer contracts, forecast/order rhythm, acceptance, delivery, collection, and credit policy.
- Margin bridge by product/customer; separate price, volume, mix, raw material, FX, and one-off effects.

Red flags:

- Revenue grows while capacity, headcount, logistics, or raw material consumption does not support it.
- Gross margin is far above peers without documented product, customer, or cost advantage.
- Inventory grows faster than orders and sales without credible delivery schedule.

## 6. Consumer / Retail / Distribution / Channel

Focus areas:

- Brand positioning, repeat purchase, SKU mix, channel mix, regional expansion, and price architecture.
- Sell-in vs sell-through, channel inventory, return policy, rebates, discounts, and distributor dependence.
- Customer acquisition cost, conversion, repeat, membership/cohort, and marketing ROI.
- Store economics if offline: same-store sales, rent, labor, shrinkage, inventory turnover, and payback.
- Supplier/contract manufacturing quality, food/drug/cosmetic/product safety where relevant.

Red flags:

- Reported sales rely on channel loading near period end.
- Rebates/returns/discounts not reflected in net revenue or margin.
- Growth plan assumes rapid store/channel expansion without unit economics proof.

## 7. Project Engineering / Environmental / Systems Integration

Focus areas:

- Backlog, signed contract, commencement, milestone, delivery, acceptance, invoicing, and collection.
- Percentage-of-completion or milestone revenue recognition; match revenue with cost and acceptance.
- Receivables aging, retention money, warranty liability, disputes, and government/customer credit.
- Project gross margin by contract; overruns, change orders, claims, subcontractors, and procurement.
- Qualification, safety, environmental permits, and performance acceptance.
- Cash-flow mismatch and working capital financing.

Red flags:

- Profit recognized before acceptance or without collectible invoices.
- Old receivables and retention money are treated as cash-like value.
- Backlog is mostly letters of intent or framework agreements without firm orders.

## 8. Asset-Heavy / Concession / Resource Models

Focus areas:

- Title, permits, pledge/mortgage, asset condition, useful life, maintenance capex, and insurance.
- Contracted cash flow: tenant/offtaker/government credit, price adjustment, termination, and renewal.
- Commodity, interest-rate, FX, utilization, and policy sensitivity.
- Environmental restoration, safety, decommissioning, and contingent liabilities.

Red flags:

- Asset value unsupported by title, permit, appraisal, or cash flow.
- Cash-flow contract is cancellable, nontransferable, or dependent on weak counterparty.
