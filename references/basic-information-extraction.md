# Basic Information Extraction

Use this before analytical writing. Noah expects DD reports to preserve and organize basic facts, not only investment opinions.

## Mandatory Tables

### 1. Material Boundary

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Material type | Local files, cloud docs, Obsidian notes, meeting minutes, transcripts, contracts, financials, public searches, specialist reports. |
| Source title/path | Use sanitized titles in user-facing reports. Keep raw private paths only when helpful and safe. |
| Date / version | Identify draft, final, signed, updated, meeting date, cutoff date. |
| Use in report | Company facts, transaction terms, financials, legal, product, management, red flags, conflicts. |
| Evidence status | Original, copy, draft, management claim, meeting note, AI transcript, third-party/specialist report, not yet opened. |
| Limitation | Missing attachments, unverified scan, OCR risk, incomplete financial schedules, source conflict. |

### 2. Entity Basics

Include when available:

- legal name, English name, aliases, brand names;
- legal representative / authorized signatory;
- establishment date, operating term, registered and operating address;
- registered capital, paid-in capital, capital contribution schedule, capital changes;
- business scope, actual business, main products/services;
- subsidiaries, branches, investees, offshore structures, platforms;
- licenses, permits, certifications, filings;
- annual filings, current operating status, required external registry checks;
- sensitive identifiers present in source materials but redacted from user-facing report.

### 3. History And Milestones

Build a date-ordered table:

| Time | Event | Evidence source | DD significance | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Cover formation background, founder project history, IP formation/transfer, financing rounds, product milestones, major contracts, regulatory approvals, disputes, and transaction milestones.

### 4. Ownership, Governance, And Incentives

Build separate tables for:

- pre-transaction cap table;
- post-transaction cap table;
- paid-in status and contribution deadlines;
- controller, concert parties, nominee holding, pledge/freeze/dispute;
- shareholder platforms and ESOP;
- board/director/supervisor/observer structure;
- reserved matters, investor vetoes, information rights, transfer restrictions, anti-dilution, liquidation preference, redemption/buyback, founder lock-up.

### 5. Transaction Facts

Include:

- investor, target, founders/sellers, existing investors;
- amount, valuation, ownership, instrument, fully diluted basis;
- prior term-sheet versions and changed terms;
- closing conditions, closing deliverables, payment mechanics;
- use of proceeds;
- reps/warranties, covenants, indemnity, termination rights;
- conflicts between draft, term sheet, signed agreement, meeting notes, and cap table.

### 6. Product, Team, And Operations Basics

Before analysis, list:

- product lines/SKUs/roadmap and stage: idea, prototype, EVT, DVT, PVT, mass production, shipped, revenue-generating;
- target users/customers, channels, pricing, contract/revenue model;
- team members, role, function, full-time status, key-person risk;
- facilities, suppliers, production/outsourcing, quality, certification, logistics;
- finance basics: cash, burn, revenue status, debt, related-party balances, budget, funding runway.

## Source Conflict Rules

- If two sources conflict, do not silently choose one. Create a source-conflict row.
- Prefer signed/original documents over term sheets, drafts, meeting summaries, or AI transcripts.
- Meeting minutes are evidence of what was said, not proof that the fact is true.
- For AI transcripts or smart summaries, mark transcription risk and use them to generate follow-up questions.
- A report may conclude with gaps, but it must show the reader exactly which facts are known, claimed, conflicting, and missing.
